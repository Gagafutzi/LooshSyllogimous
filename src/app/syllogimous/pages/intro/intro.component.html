Syllogimous

A few statements. One conclusion. Does it follow?

Every question hands you a set of relations — bigger than, before, left of, inside — and asks you to hold them together well enough to judge a claim you were never told. Nothing is written down for you. The work happens in your head, and that is the whole point.

It gets harder along several axes at once. More premises to keep track of. Premises that arrive out of order, so you cannot simply read forwards. Relations stated as the thing that isn't true. Arrangements that change after you have learned them. Eventually, conclusions you have to pick rather than judge.

The idea behind it is Relational Frame Theory: that relating things to one another is the basic move underneath most of what we call thinking, and that it can be trained like anything else. Whether that transfers beyond the task itself is genuinely unsettled — treat this as good practice at a hard thing, not a guaranteed upgrade.

You will start easy. If that is beneath you, Calibrate places you where you actually belong in about a dozen questions.
