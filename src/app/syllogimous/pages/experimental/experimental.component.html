Two ways of spacing an item rather than two kinds of item. Both replace the ordinary draw entirely while they are on, so only one of them runs at a time and the modes you picked elsewhere do not appear beside them.

Nothing here is measured. Every other mode is priced by the ability model — premises, rungs, the clock — and these two are not, because their difficulty is mostly a quantity it has no coefficient for. Runs are recorded as practice: they do not move your estimate, your tier or your premise counts. That is a deliberate choice about not corrupting the model with evidence it cannot read, not a claim that these are worth less.

One is the whole demand already: the arrangement you are answering about is never the one on screen. Past about four this stops being interference and becomes a queue — a span test on structures, which is a different thing from keeping one intact. A run is this many conclusions over {{ delayRounds + delayDepth }} screens: the first {{ delayDepth }} show an arrangement and ask nothing, because nothing is old enough yet, and the last {{ delayDepth }} ask without showing anything new while the queue drains.
Raising this measures how much you can hold. The harder demand is letting go, and that comes from objects returning later carrying a different relation — which a long run produces on its own, once it outgrows the pool of names. No ceiling. A stream is the one mode with no natural stopping point — the next premise arrives while the last question is still being resolved, so the moments where quitting is a decision are gone. This number is the only place a stop comes from, which is why it is yours and not a judgement of mine about how long anybody should sit there.
